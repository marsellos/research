Student Research, Made in R and Python

Short clips of published student research projects, each one re-run from the student’s own data and code.

Students in my group analyse their data in R or in Python, whichever suits the project. Each clip below is a published student project, re-run from the student’s own data and method. The six projects shown so far were all made in R. Every map and graph on screen is real R output, and every number was checked against the student’s poster. The students are co-authors of the work, and the full citation closes each clip.

The clips are silent and run for about twenty seconds each. Each one plays once as it comes into view; point at a clip to play it again. Click or tap a clip to watch it full screen, and click again to return.

Monitoring Earthquakes in New York (MENY)

A live earthquake map of the New York region, built with shiny and leaflet from United States Geological Survey records, and presented at the Geological Society of America. Open the live app. William Jones is now a doctoral student at Temple University, working on seismic analysis.

Jones, W., & Marsellos, A. E. (2025). Geological Society of America Abstracts with Programs, 57(3). doi:10.1130/abs/2025NE-407847

Is the sea eating away Belitung Island?

Eight digitised outlines of the island, 1990 to 2024, measured with the package sf. A quarter of the lost land lies more than one kilometre inland, and the water bodies inside the island more than doubled.

Liu, C., & Marsellos, A. E. (2026). HUSSRP Students Conference, Hofstra University. High school researcher. Poster (PDF)

Which parts of Rumson, New Jersey, flood first?

A United States Geological Survey elevation model flooded foot by foot with the package terra. Every extra foot of sea-level rise floods more land than the foot before.

Demma, G., & Marsellos, A. E. (2026). HUSSRP Students Conference, Hofstra University. High school researcher. Poster (PDF)

More heatwaves at sea?

Forty-one years of daily sea surface temperature, analysed with the package heatwaveR. The clip uses the whole 1982 to 2022 record as its baseline: 56 of the 70 heatwaves began in 2009 or later.

Cann, I., & Marsellos, A. E. (2025). Hofstra Undergraduate Research & Creativity Day. Poster (PDF)

Is the 11-year sunspot cycle steady?

Two centuries of sunspot numbers and a wavelet analysis with the package WaveletComp. The cycle is always there, but its length moves between about 10 and 11.4 years.

Colucci, D., & Marsellos, A. E. (2025). Hofstra Undergraduate Research & Creativity Day. Poster (PDF)

Do the crystals in a deformed rock point the same way?

323 quartz crystals traced on a microscope photograph, then a rose diagram drawn with ggplot2. 37 percent of the crystals point within one 30-degree range, against about 17 percent for random directions.

Persaud, D., & Marsellos, A. E. (2025). Hofstra Undergraduate Research & Creativity Day. Poster (PDF)

Learn R at Hofstra

The course Intro to R for Geoscience and Sustainability (Earth Data Analytics), 2 credits, starts from the first line of code and works with real climate, groundwater, air quality and map data. No coding experience is needed, and all course materials are provided free. It is open to undergraduate and graduate students.

In Fall 2026 the course has no catalog listing of its own, so it registers under these two codes:

  • Undergraduate students: INDEPENDENT SPECIAL PROJECTS 152-01
